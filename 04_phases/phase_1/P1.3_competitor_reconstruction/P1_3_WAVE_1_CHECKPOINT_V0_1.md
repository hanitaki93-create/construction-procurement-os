## Wave 1 strongest inherited patterns

1. low-friction supplier participation;
2. package-specific typed response structures;
3. messy/email bid ingestion alongside structured forms;
4. missing/excluded/alternate-aware bid leveling;
5. connected compare → recommend → approve workflow;
6. fast award → commitment handoff with semantic guardrails;
7. procurement schedule actuals derived from transaction progress;
8. requisition/PO/subcontract/accounting semantic rigor;
9. flexible buyout/comparison mapping;
10. field-first ordinary-material requisition and fast RFQ/PO path;

## Commercial interpretation

Wave 1 supports continued product exploration.

It does **not** prove product-market fit.

It does show that:
- dedicated construction procurement is an active software category;
- materials-only procurement can be sold as a narrower construction wedge;
- smaller construction companies purchase operational construction software at transparent recurring price points;
- enterprise platforms expose a gap in implementation gravity that a narrower procurement/commercial layer can test.

The commercialization hypothesis remains:

> an in-scope contractor may pay for procurement/commercial control after outgrowing spreadsheet/email workflows but before wanting enterprise-suite implementation.

The first commercial proof remains a live real package, not dashboard aesthetics.
